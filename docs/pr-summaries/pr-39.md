## What

Two leaks in the first live digest, both traceable to PR #38's wording:

> "...a comment-to-upvote **ratio** that marks a thread people argued over, not one they simply agreed with"

> "r/LocalLLaMA's read, **at 363 upvotes**, is that..."

The first is the model narrating its own inference. The second prints a raw count as evidence — and 363 in a 794k-member subreddit isn't even a large number, so it's cited as justification rather than because the magnitude is the story.

**Both already violated a standing output rule** ("never mention ... your own process"). PR #38's phrasing — *"marks a community DIVIDED, and the passage must say so"* — was read as "say that the ratio indicates a divided community," and because it was a **must**, that local instruction beat the global rule. My wording created the conflict.

## Fixed on both sides so they can't conflict again

- The score/comment guidance now closes by stating the figures are **INPUT ONLY**: never print a count, never name the ratio, never explain that engagement shaped the read — with reader-language alternatives spelled out ("a widely-shared post", "the window's biggest thread", "a thread people argued over"). The ambiguous "must say so" becomes "write that thread as contested".
- The **global** output rule now names upvote/comment counts explicitly, alongside the item count it already covered.

## The judgement itself was right

613 comments on 2,486 upvotes is 24.7%; 344 on 1,379 is 25%. Both sit on the one-per-four line from PR #38, and both genuinely were arguments. The weighting works — only the presentation leaked, and that's all this changes.
