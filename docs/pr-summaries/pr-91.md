## Description
Three gaps the **first real delivered tracker** exposed (digest 194, the SingularityNET RFP-governance message). All prompt-only; no code change.

## 1. Ranking — the prompt had no ranking rule at all

It said how to *cluster* stories and never how to *rank* them, so the model ranked by how much was said in the channels. It led with a website-copy dispute and buried **"final token unlock lands on the 28th"** at the fourth `Where it stands` bullet. For a holder that unlock is the most consequential item in the whole message.

Adds an explicit priority order — supply events, listings/custody, treasury, regulatory, governance rights, delivery, roadmap, team, then the state of the argument — with the corollary that matters most:

> **An unverified item high on that list outranks a confirmed item low on it.** Label it, say who claimed it, and lead with it anyway.

Burying a rumoured unlock *because* it's unconfirmed is the same failure as omitting it.

## 2. The consequence was left to be inferred — and the prompt caused that

`Report, never recommend` carried *"what it means mechanically"* as a subordinate clause, and the model played it safe. Nowhere did the message state the plain fact: **FET holders have no vote over RFP awards, the Foundation says they never did, and the site said otherwise.** That's a statement about rights attached to the asset, not advice about it.

The rule now separates the two explicitly — predictions, price targets and buy/sell framing stay banned; naming what a development changes *in fact* becomes required — and closes with:

> If the reader has to work the consequence out from your prose, you have under-delivered — not stayed safe.

An over-broad safety rule doesn't produce neutral output. It produces output that omits what the reader most needs, while *looking* careful.

## 3. Absolute dates

"the 28th" — of which month? Dates are now resolved against the item's own timestamp and written absolute. This is read every few hours and kept as a record, so a relative date decays into nonsense within a day.

## What was already working

Worth recording, since it's the baseline these changes build on. The first run held every structural contract: story-titled heading (not a rubric label), official statements attributed by name and separated from community speculation, the unlock rumour labelled unverified rather than laundered, every claim cited to a real message URL, no price talk or advice, `Where it stands` present, one Telegram message at 2433 chars, and it caught a phishing post in the official channel *and* noted no team responded in-window.
