## Description
Owner-requested design pass: from 52em up, the reading column becomes a
**sheet of paper floating above the page** instead of being the page.
Nothing inside the column moves — same 61em measure, same type scale, same
chrome, same content.

Intensity picked by the owner from three rendered options (A subtle / **B
medium** / C strong).

## What changed

All three parts live inside the existing `>=52em` block. `src/css.js` is the
only source file touched.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `--page-bg` | stops being a copy of `--bg`, becomes the gutter — `#eaedf3` light / `#0a0b0e` dark |

### The token was already here

`--page-bg` has existed since #64 (the purple "bubble") and was left behind
by #144 as a token **equal to `--bg` and referenced by no rule at all**.
This gives it its job back rather than inventing a parallel one.

### The edge is a shadow, not a border

#64 drew its card with a `1px solid var(--hairline)`. This does not. A
border states *where the card stops*; a shadow states *that the card is
above something*, which is the whole point of the ask.

### `min-height` — a real bug the render pass caught

The first cut had none, and the sheet **ended where the content ended**:
`/about`, a thin digest and an empty search result each stopped a third of
the way down the viewport and read as a truncated stub floating in the
gutter. `5em` = body's `2.5em` top + bottom padding; `box-sizing:
border-box` is global, so that is the outer height. `dvh` over `vh` costs
nothing — this block is desktop-only so the mobile dynamic-viewport problem
cannot reach it, but there is no reason to write the fragile unit.

### Why `--bg` is untouched

The larger contrast step would come from lightening the *card* rather than
darkening the *page*. But `--bg` is knocked-out ink for the active view tab,
the chips and the FAB, and it repaints the sticky day headers — moving it
would quietly restyle a dozen unrelated components. Moving `--page-bg`
touches exactly one surface.

### Why `--card-shadow` is duplicated four times

Its blacks and alphas are theme-dependent (a light-theme shadow under a
dark-theme card is invisible, and vice versa), so it cannot live once at the
top alongside `--font-prose`/`--font-data`. The comment explaining the
three-copies rule was updated from "only the COLOR variables" to
"theme-dependent variables" to stay true.

### Dark mode works differently, deliberately

A near-black page has no light left for a shadow to subtract, so in dark
mode the lift is carried by the **tone step** (`--page-bg` goes *below*
`--bg`) and the shadow only softens the boundary. Light mode is the reverse:
the shadow does most of the work.
