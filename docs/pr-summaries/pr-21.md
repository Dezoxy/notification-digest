Prompt-only change: adds one topic-keyed section (`## Geopolitics: statements outrank events`) to `prompts/digest.md`, between the news interest filter and the output contract. +25 lines, no other lines touched.

## Why

A reader review of real digests found that geopolitics coverage tracks concrete attacks and physical events — logical, since that's what headlines carry — but statements by the world's major economic and military actors are more valuable. Putin alone speaks 2–3+ times a week, and stated positions, threats, named conditions, and departures from an earlier line support further-reaching conclusions than a tally of strikes and incidents.

Decisions behind the wording:

1. **Weave into existing story clusters, no new fixed digest section.** The rule instructs lead-order *within* a geopolitics cluster (statement first, events folded beneath) and allows a consequential statement to be its own cluster. It composes with the output contract's "order sections by how much they matter" instead of fighting it.
2. **Roles + named examples, no hardcoded ranking.** The category is heads of state/government of major powers and their senior economic/military leadership; Putin, Xi, Iran's leadership, Zelensky, von der Leyen, and the German/French/UK heads of government are explicitly "illustrative examples, not a ranking" — current salience is judged from the items each window, so nothing time-sensitive is baked in.
3. **Emphasis inversion, not exclusion, and no editorializing license.** Events still get covered, just not as the lead when a real statement exists in the same story. "Significance" is bounded in-place — report what the statement commits to or changes, call it a shift only when the items show the earlier position, never add unstated consequences — pointing at the existing Hard rules, so the anti-invention constraint is restated, not weakened. The closing sentence ("a weighting rule, not a filter") keeps item scope unchanged: a geopolitics item with `source: news` still falls to the AI interest filter exactly as before.
